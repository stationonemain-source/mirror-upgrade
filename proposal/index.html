One quote, start to finish

A homeowner in Frisco wants to frame the builder-grade mirror in her bathroom. Here is what happens, screen by screen.

1

She prices the frame herself, at nine at night

Today the B304x page shows her two empty boxes and an Add to Quote button, and Google reads the frame as $0.00. On the new page she types her mirror's size and sees a price and what comes in the kit. A contractor or dealer logs in and sees their trade price instead. An unusual job? She sends a photo from the same page.

2

It lands in one inbox, with everything you need to answer

Style, size, ZIP and her photo arrive together. Quotes from the store, website chat and email all come into this one list, on your computer and on your phone. Beside each one: where they are in the sale, whether they're retail, trade or wholesale, and your team's notes.

3

She goes quiet. The follow-up doesn't, and nobody gets lost.

By the next day there's no answer. A reminder goes out in your words, then a few more over two weeks, and stops the moment she replies or says no. Email by default; texts only to customers who have agreed to them. Every customer is tagged and staged, so you can see at a glance who's talking, who's booked and who's won.

4

One email reaches every dealer

A new style, a new color, a price change: write it once in your own colors, pick "wholesale" and "trade", and send. Opens and clicks come back into the same app, so you can see which dealers looked.

And after every delivery, a short email asks for a Google review. You have one today; Barco Mirror & Glass, a mile up Addison Road, has 25.

5

A fake invoice in your name gets turned away

Two records on your domain mean anyone can send email that claims to be from order@mirrorupgrade.com. We fix them, and from then on Gmail and Outlook refuse what you didn't send.
